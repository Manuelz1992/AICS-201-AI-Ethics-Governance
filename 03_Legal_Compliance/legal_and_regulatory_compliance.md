# TASK 3: LEGAL AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

## 1. Introduction

In this section, I examined the legal and regulatory considerations affecting SecureFinance Africa's proposed AI-powered financial platform.

The assessment focuses primarily on Nigeria because the organisation is assumed to be established and operating in Nigeria. I also considered the European Union AI Act in case the organisation expands its services into the EU or its activities otherwise fall within the Act's territorial scope.

I assessed four important sources: the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and the OECD AI Principles.

An important distinction in my assessment is that legislation creates legal obligations where applicable, while international recommendations and principles provide guidance for responsible AI development and use.

## 2. Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA) is a primary legal framework governing personal data processing in Nigeria. It is particularly relevant to SecureFinance Africa because its proposed platform processes customer identity information, financial details and transaction patterns.

### 2.1 Lawful Processing and Data Protection Principles

I recommend that SecureFinance Africa establish a valid lawful basis for every category of personal data it processes.

The organisation should also observe relevant data protection principles, including:

- Lawfulness, fairness and transparency.
- Purpose limitation.
- Data minimisation.
- Accuracy.
- Storage limitation.
- Security and confidentiality.
- Accountability.

For example, the organisation should not collect additional customer information merely because the AI platform can process it. Information collected for identity verification should not automatically be reused for unrelated purposes without an appropriate lawful basis.

### 2.2 Data Privacy Impact Assessment

Section 28 of the NDPA addresses data privacy impact assessments where processing is likely to result in high risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects.

I consider a Data Privacy Impact Assessment (DPIA) necessary before introducing the proposed platform because it combines personal information processing, profiling-related activities and potentially significant automated decisions.

The assessment should identify:

- The categories of personal information being processed.
- The purposes and lawful basis of processing.
- Whether the processing is necessary and proportionate.
- Potential risks to customers.
- Proposed security and privacy safeguards.
- Measures for protecting data subject rights.

Where a DPIA indicates that high risks remain despite the proposed safeguards, the organisation should consult the Nigeria Data Protection Commission as required by Section 28(2).

### 2.3 Automated Decision-Making

Section 37 of the NDPA is especially relevant to the proposed financial risk assessment function.

It provides a right not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling, where those decisions produce legal or similarly significant effects.

The Act provides specified exceptions, including contractual necessity, authorisation by written law with suitable safeguards, and the data subject's consent.

Where an exception applies, the organisation must still implement suitable safeguards. These include providing opportunities for human intervention, allowing the individual to express their position and enabling them to contest the decision.

I therefore recommend that SecureFinance Africa avoid treating automated financial recommendations as unquestionable decisions. Customers should have an accessible review and challenge process.

### 2.4 Security and Accountability

I recommend appropriate technical and organisational security measures to protect customer information against unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration and loss.

These should include encryption, access restrictions, secure authentication, audit logging, staff training and an incident response procedure.

The organisation should also identify its data controller and processor responsibilities and ensure that relevant third-party providers operate under appropriate contractual and security arrangements.

### 2.5 Application to SecureFinance Africa

I consider the NDPA directly relevant to the organisation's assumed Nigerian operations. The organisation should complete its data protection compliance assessment before deployment and maintain evidence of its lawful processing, security and accountability measures.

## 3. European Union Artificial Intelligence Act

The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, establishes a risk-based regulatory framework for AI systems.

Although SecureFinance Africa is assumed to operate primarily in Nigeria, the Act may become relevant if the organisation places an AI system on the EU market, provides it within the scope of the Act, or uses its outputs in the EU under the applicable territorial rules.

Therefore, I would not assume that every Nigerian AI activity is automatically governed by the EU AI Act.

### 3.1 Risk-Based Classification

The EU AI Act distinguishes between prohibited AI practices, high-risk systems and systems subject to transparency obligations.

For SecureFinance Africa, the most important potential classification concerns financial risk assessment.

Under Annex III, point 5(b), AI systems intended to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score are generally classified as high-risk, with an exception for systems used for detecting financial fraud.

This distinction is important. A system that assesses an individual's creditworthiness should not be treated in the same way as a system whose purpose is solely to detect financial fraud.

The organisation must examine the actual intended purpose and functionality of each AI component before determining its classification.

### 3.2 High-Risk AI Requirements

Where the Act applies and the financial risk assessment component qualifies as high-risk, relevant requirements may include:

- Risk management throughout the AI system lifecycle.
- Data governance and appropriate dataset quality.
- Technical documentation and record-keeping.
- Transparency and information for deployers.
- Human oversight.
- Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity.
- Appropriate conformity assessment and post-market monitoring obligations.

I recommend that SecureFinance Africa maintain documentation demonstrating how its financial assessment models are developed, tested, monitored and controlled.

### 3.3 Identity Verification and Biometrics

The organisation also proposes customer identity verification.

I distinguish biometric verification from remote biometric identification. Under Annex III, the AI Act excludes systems intended solely to confirm that a specific person is the person they claim to be from the relevant remote biometric identification category.

However, the actual purpose and functionality must be examined. Other biometric uses may fall within different regulatory classifications.

### 3.4 Application to SecureFinance Africa

For its assumed Nigerian-only operations, the EU AI Act should be treated as a conditional future compliance consideration rather than an automatically applicable law.

Before entering the European market or serving customers within the Act's territorial scope, the organisation should obtain a documented classification assessment and identify its obligations as a provider, deployer or other relevant AI actor.

The organisation should also monitor the Act's phased implementation and applicable amendments before any EU deployment.

## 4. UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

UNESCO adopted its Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence on 23 November 2021.

Unlike national legislation, this Recommendation is a non-binding international normative instrument. It encourages member states and other stakeholders to apply its principles voluntarily and through appropriate national measures.

I consider it relevant to SecureFinance Africa because it provides an ethical foundation for responsible AI development.

The following principles are particularly relevant:

### 4.1 Fairness and Non-Discrimination

UNESCO encourages AI actors to minimise discriminatory and biased outcomes throughout the AI lifecycle.

I recommend that SecureFinance Africa regularly examine whether its system produces unequal outcomes across relevant customer groups.

### 4.2 Privacy and Data Protection

The Recommendation emphasises protecting privacy and personal information throughout the AI lifecycle.

I recommend that the organisation integrate privacy-by-design measures and conduct appropriate privacy impact assessments.

### 4.3 Human Oversight

UNESCO recognises that AI systems should not replace ultimate human responsibility and accountability.

I therefore recommend that significant financial decisions remain subject to meaningful human review and appropriate escalation procedures.

### 4.4 Transparency and Explainability

Customers should receive appropriate information when AI influences decisions affecting them.

I recommend that SecureFinance Africa maintain understandable explanations, decision records and channels through which customers can challenge potentially incorrect outcomes.

### 4.5 Application to SecureFinance Africa

Although the Recommendation does not independently create the same enforceable obligations as Nigerian legislation, I consider it a useful benchmark for evaluating whether the organisation's AI practices respect human dignity, fairness, privacy and accountability.

## 5. OECD AI Principles

The OECD AI Principles are an international recommendation designed to promote innovative and trustworthy AI while respecting human rights and democratic values.

The Principles were adopted in 2019 and updated in May 2024. They are non-binding and provide guidance for governments and AI stakeholders.

I identified the following principles as particularly relevant to SecureFinance Africa.

### 5.1 Inclusive Growth and Well-Being

The platform should provide financial services without unnecessarily excluding customers because of unfair or discriminatory automated outcomes.

### 5.2 Human Rights, Fairness and Privacy

The organisation should respect customers' rights, protect personal information and avoid discriminatory practices.

### 5.3 Transparency and Explainability

The organisation should provide relevant information about the system's capabilities, limitations and significant decisions.

### 5.4 Robustness, Security and Safety

The platform should be tested and monitored to ensure that it remains reliable, secure and resilient against errors and attacks.

### 5.5 Accountability

Clear responsibilities should be assigned to the people and teams involved in developing, deploying and monitoring the platform.

### 5.6 Application to SecureFinance Africa

I recommend using the OECD Principles as an additional governance benchmark when developing internal AI policies, monitoring performance and reviewing the organisation's accountability arrangements.

They should complement, rather than replace, applicable Nigerian and international legal obligations.

## 6. Comparison of the Four Regulatory and Ethical Sources

| Source | Legal or normative status | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 | Binding Nigerian legislation | Directly relevant to assumed Nigerian personal data processing |
| EU AI Act | Binding EU regulation within its applicable scope | Conditional on territorial scope and system classification |
| UNESCO Recommendation | Non-binding international recommendation | Ethical guidance on human rights, fairness and oversight |
| OECD AI Principles | Non-binding international recommendation | Guidance on trustworthy AI and accountability |

I consider this distinction important because an organisation should not describe voluntary international recommendations as if they were enforceable national statutes.

## 7. Compliance Recommendations

Based on my assessment, I recommend that SecureFinance Africa:

1. Complete a documented NDPA compliance assessment before processing customer information.
2. Conduct a DPIA before introducing high-risk personal data processing.
3. Establish lawful bases and clear purposes for all personal data processing.
4. Provide appropriate safeguards for significant automated decisions.
5. Maintain accessible human review and customer challenge procedures.
6. Assess the EU AI Act's territorial scope and classify each AI component before EU-related deployment.
7. Adopt UNESCO and OECD principles as internal ethical governance benchmarks.
8. Maintain audit records, technical documentation and regular compliance reviews.
9. Establish appropriate security and third-party data protection controls.
10. Review regulatory changes and update governance policies when necessary.

## 8. Conclusion

My assessment indicates that SecureFinance Africa must treat legal compliance and ethical governance as essential requirements for its proposed AI platform.

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 is particularly relevant to the organisation's assumed domestic operations because of its processing of personal and financial information. The EU AI Act may become relevant if the organisation enters its territorial scope, particularly where its financial risk assessment system evaluates the creditworthiness of natural persons.

UNESCO and OECD provide additional guidance on fairness, privacy, transparency, human oversight and accountability. Although these recommendations are not directly equivalent to binding legislation, they provide useful standards for responsible AI governance.

I recommend that SecureFinance Africa complete its legal and ethical compliance assessments before deployment and maintain continuous monitoring as the platform develops.
